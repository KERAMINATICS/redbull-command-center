03 — how much trust to put in a forecast

Forecast benchmark

A Random Forest demand model is only worth using if it beats the simplest reasonable alternative — repeating what happened in the same month last year. Both were scored on 6 held-out months.

Random Forest Prior-year seasonal naive
Random Forest: MAE 10,585, RMSE 15,828. Seasonal naive: MAE 30,104, RMSE 35,753.
Random Forest cut mean absolute error by roughly two-thirds versus the naive baseline — evidence the model is learning real structure in the data, not just curve-fitting. See the model card for validation detail and limitations.

05 — a measurement problem, not a budget problem

Sports & event activation ROI

Spend on six sponsorship-style activations was resampled 2,000 times under three attribution assumptions — conservative, base, and upside — to see how uncertain the ROI estimate really is.

5th percentile Median 95th percentile
Conservative: -99.6% to -99.4%. Base: -99.2% to -98.7%. Upside: -98.9% to -98.3%.
ROI is negative across all 2,000 resamples in every attribution scenario — with roughly 0% probability of a positive result. That's not evidence sponsorships lose money; it's evidence that measuring only direct, immediately-attributed revenue is the wrong way to value them. A real assessment needs brand-lift and assisted-conversion tracking, not a bigger attribution multiplier.
